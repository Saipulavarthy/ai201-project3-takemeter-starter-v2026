# Community notes: r/LocalLLaMA

Community: r/LocalLLaMA, a public subreddit about running AI models locally on personal hardware. I read about 35 posts from the Hot feed in October 2026.

Distinction 1: measured numbers. Some posts report numbers the author measured or that can be checked, such as tokens per second on a named GPU, VRAM and RAM sizes, quantization levels, context lengths, or pull request numbers. The BitRun post lists speeds for three specific CPUs, and the QFN Q_2 post gives exact flags and hardware. Other posts mention a price or a hardware name with no measurement behind it. Most posts have no numbers at all.

Distinction 2: evidence for a claim. Some posts make a claim and describe how they got there, like the author who argued that tool templates matter more than models and described their test setup and failure counts. Others make a confident claim with nothing offered, or just state that something exists or happened.

Distinction 3: reacting vs. working something out. Some posts are an immediate emotional response to news, such as shock at GPU prices or reactions to a story, where the feeling is the content. Others slow down and ask what evidence would settle a question, like the post asking whether Strata's speed costs quality.

Distinction 4: question detail. Many posts are questions, and they vary from one line ("What's the most powerful model I can run on an A2000 12GB?") to detailed setups listing the motherboard, GPUs, constraints, and options already considered. The detailed ones give readers something to work with.
